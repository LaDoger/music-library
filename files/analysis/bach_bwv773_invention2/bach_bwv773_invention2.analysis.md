# Johann Sebastian Bach: Two-Part Invention No. 2 in C minor

Source: `files/scores/bach_bwv773_invention2.mid`, absolute quarters [0, 108.0]; 4/4, c, opening ♩≈64.0. MIDI is referenced, not copied.

A two-voice canon in two halves. Bars 1–11: the RH subject is followed two bars later by the LH an octave below, moving from C minor to E♭ major. Bars 11–20: the same canon transposed to G minor with the voices exchanged (LH leads), moving to B♭. A two-bar sequence (21–22) leads back to C minor, where the subject returns over its own counter-line (23) and is imitated in the bass (25) before a PAC on a bare C octave.

## Story and key journey

- Bars 1–10: **Canon I (c → E♭)**. RH subject, LH canon at the lower octave from bar 3; ii–V7–I cadences settle in E♭. Mood: brooding, restless.
- Bars 11–20: **Canon II, voices exchanged (g → B♭)**. Bars 1–10 a fifth lower with LH leading; arrives in B♭ at bar 17. Mood: searching, darker.
- Bars 21–22: **Sequence back to C minor**. D7 and a stepped sequence lead to G7/B; LH scale falls to the dominant. Mood: tense, driving.
- Bars 23–27: **Tonic return and close**. Subject in RH, then in LH two octaves lower; vii°7–V–i and a bare final octave. Mood: resolute, sombre.

## Tricks checked against the notes

- Bars 1–11: a strict canon at the octave with a two-bar delay. One line is enough material for the whole first half.
- Bar 11: the canon is restarted with the voices exchanged and transposed to G minor (invertible counterpoint); bars 11–20 mirror bars 1–10.
- Bars 4–10 and 14–20: repeated ii–V7–I cells (Fm–B♭7–E♭, Cm–F7–B♭) make each relative-major key area sound settled.
- Bars 22 and 24: long stepwise bass descents drive into the tonic arrivals.
- Bar 27: ends on a bare C octave, leaving out the minor third.
